Bulleh Shah, whose given name is usually recorded as Abdullah Shah, was born around 1680 at Uch into a Sayyid family. His father, Shah Muhammad Darwaish, later moved the family to Pandoke, near Kasur. Kasur is where Bulleh Shah spent most of his life and where his shrine stands. Most sources give his death as 1757; Christopher Shackle gives 1758.

He became a disciple of Shah Inayat Qadiri of Lahore, a man of the Arain caste. Choosing a teacher considered lower-born than his own Sayyid family angered his relatives, and the episode runs through his poetry. Local clerics are said to have refused him burial inside the town, and Kasur later grew around his tomb.

His kafis are short sung lyrics built on a refrain. They mock ritual, book-learning and the authority of clerics, and turn again and again to the question of the self. He also left dohre, a Baran Maah, an Athwara, gandhan and siharfis.
