Bulleh Shah, born Abdullah Shah, is the best-known poet of the Punjabi Sufi tradition. Most accounts place his early life at Uch and his family's later settlement near Kasur, where he spent most of his life and where his shrine stands.

He became a disciple of Shah Inayat Qadiri, a gardener of the Arain caste in Lahore. Choosing a teacher from a caste considered lower than his own Sayyid family scandalised his relatives, and the episode runs through his poetry.

His kafis are short sung lyrics built on a refrain. They mock ritual, book-learning and the authority of clerics, and turn again and again to the question of the self. They remain part of the living repertoire of qawwals and folk singers on both sides of the border.
