Hashim Shah (also spelled Hasham) came from Jagdev Kalan in Amritsar district, where his father's grave is still visited. His dates are uncertain: his birth is given as 1735, 1752 or 1753 and his death as 1823 or 1843, and accounts also differ on where he died, naming Ajnala or Tharpal, now in Pakistan.

He worked as a carpenter, and also followed his family's calling as a hakim and spiritual guide, until Maharaja Ranjit Singh and his courtiers became his patrons. After that he gave himself to Sufi practice and poetry, writing in Punjabi as well as in other languages.

He is remembered above all for his *Sassi Punnun*, the qissa of the lovers of the desert, and for his dohras. His *Sohni Mahiwal* and *Shirin Farhad* are also well known.
